# rubric: lens (layer 1, multi)

the job or vantage point of the protagonist relative to crime work.
not the relationship to power (that is authority_stance) and not the
mechanism (that is mystery_mechanism). a work can carry several lenses:
psych is private-eye and consulting-detective at once. up to 2 primary
and up to 3 secondary when tagging (though layer 1 carries no evidence
requirement, one line of why is always welcome).

## values

### cop
- definition: municipal or city police, the street-level institution.
- how to observe: the lead works cases as employed police in a town
  or city department.
- counterexample: a county officer outside a city department. that is
  sheriff, not cop.

### sheriff
- definition: county or rural law enforcement.
- how to observe: the jurisdiction is a county or spread-out rural
  area; the office is elected or county-run, not a city precinct.
- counterexample: a city detective in a small town. check the
  institution, not the population.

### fed
- definition: federal agent (fbi, atf, dea, ncis-style agencies).
- how to observe: the lead carries federal jurisdiction and a national
  or multi-state scope.
- counterexample: a federal-flavored task force staffed by local cops.
  tag the cops, not the task force's letterhead.

### intelligence
- definition: intelligence-community crime work (analysis, surveillance
  desks, spooks affiliated with the services).

### spy
- definition: espionage-forward work where the mission is the point,
  not the case.

### private-eye
- definition: a licensed or practicing private investigator, paid by
  clients.
- how to observe: a pi office, clients, casework for hire.
- counterexample: an amateur who never takes money for it. that is
  amateur or consulting-detective depending on who asks for help.

### consulting-detective
- definition: a civilian detective consulted by law enforcement or
  clients, without being employed by either.
- how to observe: no rank, no badge, brought in for their ability;
  sherlock, ludwig, monk, the mentalist.
- counterexample: a criminal doing the consulting. that is
  criminal-consultant.

### criminal-consultant
- definition: a criminal consulting for law enforcement, usually under
  constraint or deal (white collar).
- how to observe: the lead's criminal skill is the tool; the
  arrangement is conditional and reversible.
- counterexample: a reformed criminal now properly employed. tag the
  current role, not the backstory.

### forensic-scientist
- definition: the protagonist focus is forensic science (pathologist,
  anthropologist, criminalist driving the case).
- how to observe: the lead's expertise opens and closes cases; bones.

### forensic-support
- definition: accompanying csi-style roles inside law enforcement.
- how to observe: the forensic role serves an existing investigation
  rather than leading it.

### amateur
- definition: an unpaid civilian who keeps finding crimes (jessica
  fletcher, father brown).
- counterexample: a civilian hired or formally consulted. that is
  consulting-detective.

### journalist / military / firefighter / legal / politician
- definition: the lead's vantage point on crime work is the press, the
  armed services, the fire service, the law, or elected office. legal
  overlaps crime_relation defending; tag legal when the practice of law
  is the lens itself (jag is military and legal).
- military pairs freely with the others: ncis is fed and military;
  jag is military and legal. tag both lenses, not the stronger one.
- politician covers crime work seen from elected or appointed office:
  the political consequence is the vantage (a police commissioner who
  is the story's political animal is still cop; a mayor or council
  lead navigating the crime story is politician).
