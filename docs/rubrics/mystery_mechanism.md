# rubric: mystery_mechanism (layer 1, multi, crime module)

how the mystery, when there is one, actually works. this is the axis
that separates the two kinds of crime fans in the readme: the
institution-cop fan and the puzzle fan can both be watching "crime
shows" while sharing nothing here.

a work can carry more than one mechanism across its run, and some
episodes invert. tag the dominant engine of the show, the one a viewer
would name if asked "what kind of mystery is this".

## values

### none
- definition: there is no puzzle. the work is a cop show, a response
  show, a chase show: cases resolve through procedure, response and
  danger, not through deduction from clues. the rookie, blue bloods.
- how to observe: ask what the viewer is doing during an episode. if
  they are never invited to wonder who did it, the mechanism is none.
- boundary: none is a real value, not a gap. a show with no whodunit
  structure is tagged `none`, not left untagged, so the catalog can
  answer "whodunits without cop-lens" and "cop shows without
  whodunits" with the same arithmetic.
- counterexample: a procedural whose episodes happen to hinge on a
  hidden culprit who is unmasked by evidence. that is whodunit even if
  the cops never say the word.

### whodunit
- definition: a hidden culprit is revealed by assembling evidence.
  the viewer is invited to wonder who.
- counterexample: the killer shown in the opening scene. that is
  howdunnit territory.

### howdunnit
- definition: the culprit is known early; the episode is about proving
  it, catching them in it, or watching the trap close. columbo,
  elsbeth, lots of ludwig.
- counterexample: a reveal that depends on guessing identity rather
  than method. that is whodunit.

### whydunnit
- definition: the who and often the how are clear; the question is the
  motive, the psychology underneath.
- counterexample: motive delivered as a throwaway line at the arrest.
  that is whodunit with a speech at the end.

### proving-it
- definition: the lead knows and must convince a skeptic: a boss, a
  jury, a board, the law.
- counterexample: the proof arrives by confession after pressure.
  convenience is not proving-it.

### inverted
- definition: the crime is shown first, the episode replays it or
  walks its edges until the sides meet.
- counterexample: cold opens that tease but hide the culprit.

### caper
- definition: the crime is the protagonist's project and the pleasure
  is the execution. heists, cons, long cons.
- counterexample: a heist the heroes interrupt. who the thieves are
  is the question, so whodunnit.

## the rookie test

if the work is about the job rather than the puzzle, mechanism is
`none`. the rookie is the canonical case: policing as vocation, danger
and growth, no viewer-side deduction at all.
