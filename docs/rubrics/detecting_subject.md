# rubric: detecting_subject (layer 1, multi)

what is being investigated. the subject of the crime, not the crime's
severity or its lens. most works carry several; tag up to 2 primary
(subjects the work is actually about) and up to 3 secondary (present,
recurring, but not the spine).

## how to tag

- ask: what cases does this work spend its time on?
- a murder inside an organized-crime story is a beat, not the subject:
  the wire has murders, the subject is drug-trade + organized-crime +
  corruption.
- tag from the cases, not from marketing.

## values

### white-collar
- definition: desk crime by professionals: laundering, embezzlement,
  insider trading, asset seizure, corporate fraud.
- owner example: hidden assets (the irish cab unit exists for this:
  follow the money, seize it).
- counterexample: fraud committed by a desperate nobody for survival.
  that is fraud, and the desperation is the story.

### corruption
- definition: the institution itself is the criminal: bent cops,
  bought officials, rot from inside.
- examples: line of duty, hidden assets, the wire's hamsterdam and
  city hall threads.
- counterexample: one bad apple caught in an otherwise trusted
  institution. tag corruption only when the rot is the subject, not
  when a single villain wears a badge.

### organized-crime
- definition: a durable criminal organization as subject: family,
  syndicate, cartel, club.
- examples: the wire's organizations, sons of anarchy, boardwalk
  empire, power.
- counterexample: a lone crew pulled together for one job. that is a
  caper, and the crew is an ensemble, not an institution.

### drug-trade
- definition: production, distribution, or empire-building on drugs.
- examples: the wire (with organized-crime), breaking bad (with
  organized-crime as it grows), power.
- counterexample: a single user's addiction story. that is drama with
  drugs, not a drug-trade investigation.

### trafficking
- definition: human trafficking networks, smuggling of people.
- counterexample: one kidnapping. that is a specific crime, usually
  disappearance or murder with the case as the spine.

### murder
- definition: homicide as the spine of the caseload.
- counterexample: one murder that opens an espionage plot. subject is
  espionage; murder is the door.

### financial
- definition: money crimes without the white-collar posture:
  extortion, robbery, ransom, heists that are about the money.
- boundary with white-collar: white-collar is committed from a desk
  by someone respectable; financial is any crime about money. when
  both apply, tag both.

### fraud
- definition: deception as the mechanism: cons, forgery, identity.
- examples: white collar (the show) is fraud + art crime.
- boundary: a con is fraud; tag white-collar only when the fraud is
  professional-scale business crime.

### cybercrime
- definition: crime whose mechanism is computational: hacking,
  crypto theft, digital espionage adjacent to crime.

### cold-case
- definition: old cases reopened.
- counterexample: a case that is merely months old. cold-case is
  about time and silence.

### espionage
- definition: state-on-state spying and its crimes.
- counterexample: corporate spying. that is white-collar or fraud
  unless states are involved.

### other
- definition: the subject exists but no value fits. propose a new
  value in docs/open-questions.md rather than stretching one.
