# rubric: authority_stance (layer 2, single)

the protagonist's relationship to power: institutional authority and how
the lead stands toward it. this is not their job title and not their
competence. the lens (cop, consultant, amateur) is the vantage point;
authority_stance is the stance toward the institution. this axis carries
weight 2.0 in scoring, and it is where "the rookie" and "psych" separate
even though both are crime procedurals.

## how to tag

- exactly one primary, no secondaries (single-select).
- ask: when the institution and the protagonist disagree, whose side
  does the story frame as right?
- look for the recurring friction, not the pilot episode.
- if no meaningful institutional power relationship drives the work,
  use unknown (example: a small-town family drama with no institution
  in play).
- a consultant lens can pair with several stances: tag the stance, not
  the job.

## values

### institution-believer
- definition: the lead trusts and works within the institution, and the
  story treats the institution as fundamentally good even when flawed.
- how to observe: the lead defends the system against criticism;
  failures are attributed to bad apples, not the structure; the
  institution's approval is the reward.
- boundary: doubt episodes are allowed. tag the dominant stance of the
  run, and note a real shift as a season override if the rubric's other
  axes shift too.
- counterexample: a cop lead whose arc is exposing the department's rot
  and who never reassures the audience the system works. inside the
  institution, but a cynic.

### institution-cynic
- definition: inside the system, but believes it is broken; keeps
  working within it anyway, often bitterly.
- how to observe: recurring complaints about bureaucracy or corruption;
  cases solved despite the institution; resignation played for irony.
- boundary: the cynic still works inside. total exit is a different
  stance.
- counterexample: a lead who starts cynical and converts to believer by
  season's end (tag the dominant stance; the convert is usually
  institution-believer with the arc as evidence).

### adjacent-consultant
- definition: works alongside the institution by choice or contract,
  keeps independence, and is treated as a peer, neither dismissed nor
  opposed.
- how to observe: invited in, given access, respected; free to leave;
  friction is procedural, not existential.
- boundary: this is the mutual-respect version of outside help.
- counterexample: a consultant the police dismiss and mock until
  results force them to concede. that is outsider-underestimated.

### outsider-underestimated
- definition: wants in or works with the institution, is dismissed, and
  wins by seeing differently.
- how to observe: explicit dismissal scenes; the vindication pattern;
  the institution needs them but does not want them.
- boundary: the dismissal must be real and recurring, not a formality.
- counterexample: a brilliant outsider treated with respect from the
  first scene. underestimated in premise, never in practice.

### adversarial-vigilante
- definition: stands outside and against the institution; takes
  enforcement into their own hands.
- how to observe: acts the institution cannot or will not take; the
  conflict with official power is the drama; cooperation is tactical
  and temporary.
- boundary: bending rules while cooperating is not this. vigilantes act
  where the system is refused or replaced.
- counterexample: a rogue-ish operator who still hands every case to
  the police and answers to them. colorful, but adjacent or outsider.

### criminal-protagonist
- definition: the lead commits the crimes the story is about; the
  institution is an antagonist or a hazard.
- how to observe: the narrative asks the audience to root for the crime
  to succeed; law enforcement is the obstacle.
- boundary: crime_relation (layer 1) covers committing; this stance is
  about the power relationship.
- counterexample: an innocent lead fighting a wrongful conviction. the
  crime exists, but the stance toward the system is cynic or believer,
  not criminal.
