# rubric: fantasy (layer 2, multi)

the fantasy is the wish the work is selling: the recurring payoff the
audience is invited to enjoy. not a theme, not a tone. the question is
"what does this grant me, again and again?" this axis carries weight 2.0
in scoring, so tag it carefully and from evidence.

## how to tag

- ask what payoff appears in most episodes or chapters, not what the
  work is about in one strong hour.
- primary: the promise made almost every time. up to 2 primary.
- secondary: recurring, but not the engine of the show. up to 3 secondary.
- fantasy is independent of tone and world: a dark world can sell
  comfort (the case is solved, the village is safe again); a bright
  world can sell transgression.
- if no recurring wish is identifiable, use unknown. do not stretch.
- open question 11: this axis may split later into agency/belonging
  clusters. tag the listed values as they are.

## values

### competence
- definition: the pleasure of watching mastery: the work lingers on
  displays of skill and rewards expertise.
- how to observe: the camera dwells on the protagonist doing the thing
  brilliantly; the payoff is the demonstration.
- owner example: the spy lens is full of competence porn, but slow horses
  is the opposite: the joy is the rejects muddling through and turning
  out to be right. tag the fantasy the work sells, not the one its genre
  is famous for. slow horses is spy-lens with competence absent; what it
  sells is underdog-vindication and belonging.
- boundary: a competent lead is not competence fantasy. nolan in the
  rookie is competent; the show sells growth and belonging, not
  demonstrations of mastery. the fantasy axis asks what the work
  invites you to enjoy.
- counterexample: slow horses (spy lens, competence fantasy absent:
  the pleasure is the failure's vindication, not the master's display).
### justice
- definition: the wish that wrongs are righted and consequences arrive.
- how to observe: the guilty pay, restitution is the climax, the story
  frames the settling of accounts as the satisfying payoff, even when
  the route is unrealistic.
- boundary: justice can coexist with a broken system; what matters is
  the narrative delivers consequences, through the institution or
  around it.
- counterexample: a crime story that ends ambiguous or lets the culprit
  walk for thematic reasons (prestige-era serialized crime). there the
  payoff is comprehension, not justice.

### puzzle
- definition: the wish to match wits with the story.
- how to observe: fair-play clueing; the audience is invited to solve
  ahead of the reveal; the case is framed as a game with a knowable
  answer.
- boundary: puzzle is about the viewer's participation. a twisty story
  that never lets the audience play along is not this.
- counterexample: a solution that arrives via a last-act witness or a
  confession extracted off screen. mystery setting, but the fantasy
  was never offered to the audience.

### belonging
- definition: the wish to be taken in by a group, family, or place that
  sees you.
- how to observe: arcs resolve into acceptance; the group functions as
  shelter; leaving or losing the group is the threat.
- boundary: belonging needs warmth and permanence, not just people
  sharing a room.
- counterexample: a workplace of interchangeable colleagues with no
  intimacy. ensemble, no belonging.

### comfort
- definition: the wish for a safe, restorative world where nothing
  truly bad happens.
- how to observe: threats stay mild; resolution restores the status
  quo; recurring rituals (the cafe, the town meeting, the pub) are the
  point.
- boundary: comfort is about the world being safe, cozy (tone) is about
  warmth. they often travel together but comfort can exist in a
  sharp-tongued show.
- counterexample: a cozy-set series where a core character is suddenly
  killed for shock. the promise was broken or never made.

### transgression
- definition: the wish to break the rules and enjoy it, or to watch
  someone who does.
- how to observe: the lead bends or breaks rules and the narrative
  rewards or delights in it; the audience is aligned with the rule
  breaker.
- boundary: the story must side with the transgression, not merely
  depict it.
- counterexample: rule-breaking punished as a moral lesson. depicted,
  but not sold.

### underdog-vindication
- definition: the wish that the dismissed person is proven right.
- how to observe: a repeated pattern of doubt, then vindication; the
  "told you so" is the climax; being underestimated is the setup, being
  right is the payoff.
- boundary: this needs the pattern, not one triumphant finale. if the
  vindication happened once and never again, do not tag it.
- counterexample: an underdog lead who keeps losing or is never doubted
  in the first place. the word "underdog" is not the pattern.
