# rubric: romance_dynamic (layer 2, multi, optional)

the relational shape of the lead's romance: who the two people are to
each other before they are lovers. tagged only when a romance exists
(romantic_arc other than none). this axis exists so compound affinities
like "whodunit plus partners-to-lovers slow burn" are writable, which is
the owner's stated taste pattern (ncis tony/ziva, psych shawn/jules,
the mentalist patrick/lisbon).

## how to tag

- up to 2 primary and up to 3 secondary (multi-select).
- skip the axis entirely when romantic_arc is none.
- tag the dynamic the narrative foregrounds, not every arrangement
  the pairing passes through.

## values

### partners-to-lovers
- definition: the pair work side by side (cases, jobs, missions)
  before romance enters.
- how to observe: the professional bond is established and repeated
  well before the romance; the audience watches the partnership
  become intimacy.
- counterexample: two leads who share a workplace but the story never
  foregrounds them as a working pair. that is friends-to-lovers or
  nothing.

### friends-to-lovers
- definition: an established friendship becomes romance.
- how to observe: seasons or volumes of platonic intimacy first.
- counterexample: a pair introduced as a couple. no dynamic is tagged.

### enemies-to-lovers
- definition: the pair starts in opposition or genuine antagonism.
- how to observe: the early conflict is real and the conversion is
  the arc the story savors.
- counterexample: a pair with banter but no real opposition. that is
  partners- or friends-to-lovers.

### forbidden
- definition: an external rule or vow keeps the pair apart (vocation,
  duty, family, law).
- how to observe: the obstacle is structural, not circumstantial, and
  the story treats the boundary as the tension.
- counterexample: distance or bad timing. circumstantial obstacles are
  not forbidden.

### reunion
- definition: a past romance returns years later.
- how to observe: history between the pair predates the work's present
  and drives it.
- counterexample: a new pairing that starts in the present. reunion
  needs the past to matter.

### second-chance
- definition: a failed romance inside the work's timeline is
  reattempted.
- how to observe: the pairing formed, failed, and the reattempt is
  the arc.
- counterexample: a breakup-and-repeat cycle, which is
  romantic_arc/on-off-cycle territory rather than a dynamic.
