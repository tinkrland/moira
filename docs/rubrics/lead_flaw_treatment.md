# rubric: lead_flaw_treatment (layer 2, single)

what the narrative does with the lead's flaws. not whether flaws exist:
what the story does with them. this is the axis that separates a
surface trait from a meaningful affinity. "abrasive protagonist" is a
trait; "an abrasive woman whose story never demands she be corrected"
is a fact about the narrative's treatment, and only the second one
tells you what the work is selling (see scenario s3 and concept.md).

## how to tag

- exactly one primary, no secondaries (single-select).
- watch the flaw's arc, not the flaw: is it a problem to fix, a
  tolerated fact of life, a celebrated feature, or not engaged at all?
- the judgment belongs to the narrative, not other characters: allies
  may complain while the show clearly rewards the behavior.
- if the lead has no flaw the narrative engages with, that is
  no-flaw-focus, not a compliment.
- if you cannot tell from actual episodes, use unknown.

## values

### flaw-corrected
- definition: the story treats the flaw as a problem to fix; arcs push
  growth and the lead changes.
- how to observe: interventions, consequences traced to the flaw,
  a measurably different protagonist over the run; the correction is
  presented as progress.
- boundary: one setback episode is not an arc. correction must be the
  pattern.
- counterexample: a flawed lead whose flaws cause chaos and are played
  for comedy with no growth demanded. shown, but tolerated.

### flaw-tolerated
- definition: the narrative shows the flaw and lets it persist; the
  world accommodates it, the story neither punishes it as the central
  problem nor applauds it as the appeal.
- how to observe: the flaw persists across seasons without a growth arc;
  relationships bend around it; no episode treats fixing it as the
  point.
- boundary: tolerated is not invisible. the show must engage with the
  flaw, just without a verdict.
- counterexample: the flaw is the running joke the show obviously
  loves and rewards. engaged with, but celebrated.

### flaw-celebrated
- definition: the flaw is the appeal; the narrative rewards it and the
  work admires it through its own framing.
- how to observe: the flaw wins cases, friends, or success; other
  characters or the storytelling explicitly admire it; removing it
  would break the show's promise.
- boundary: celebration is the story's stance, not the fandom's.
- counterexample: the flaw persists and the world adapts, but the
  story never frames it as admirable. tolerated.

### no-flaw-focus
- definition: the lead is not characterized through a flaw the
  narrative engages with; failings are incidental, off-screen, or
  unmotivated by design.
- how to observe: no recurring narrative beats about the lead's
  personal failings; the lead's inner life is not the material.
- boundary: a running-gag flaw the show plays with is engagement: that
  is tolerated (or celebrated), not this value.
- counterexample: a competent lead whose single flaw is a joke the show
  revisits every episode. engaged with, so not no-flaw-focus.

## note for compound affinities

this axis is what makes a compound affinity like "abrasive manner +
female lead + flaw tolerated" expressible. the trait says the lead is
abrasive; this axis says whether the story punishes, shrugs at, or
applauds it. users will combine them (taste-context.md). keep the
boundary between the three engaged values sharp when tagging.
