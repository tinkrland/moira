# the problem

## the blend

every app you use maintains one profile of you. your bank app, your study
notes, your writing app, your chat assistant - each blends whatever it
sees into a single gray "user". the harm today is creepiness; the harm
tomorrow is that this blended profile is the only version of you any
system knows.

## the agent era makes it worse

you are about to hand ai agents more access than you have ever handed
any software: your files, your calendar, your mail, your money. the
current model for that is a giant long-lived token with access to
everything, held by a process you did not write, forever. "just give me
access" is the only offer on the table.

## the specific failures

- **no separation.** what you're studying is nobody's business at work;
  your investor persona's numbers are not your creative persona's
  material. today you solve this with separate accounts, separate
  browsers, separate lives - or you don't, and it blends.
- **no granularity.** an assistant that can read everything for
  convenience reasons will read everything, including what you never
  intended, because there is no way to say "this, for this, until
  tuesday".
- **no memory of sharing.** you cannot answer "what did this app
  actually take from me, and when?" there is no record you own.
- **no real deletion.** "delete" hides rows from you and keeps them for
  everyone else. keys still exist; the data still decrypts.
- **provider text becomes truth.** platforms export your "preferences" -
  engagement telemetry dressed as taste - and downstream systems treat
  it as what you believe.
- **no audit spine.** context is shared invisibly: syncs, integrations,
  "connected apps" - none of it leaves an owner-readable trail of who
  got what.

## who feels it

anyone who runs multiple lives that all matter: students building
startups, professionals with creative sides, people managing health or
finance context they don't want in every chat window, anyone whose work
means clients see a different face than friends do.

## why now

agents are the first software class that actually needs to read your
context to work. so this is the moment the sharing model gets decided -
permanently, at the protocol level - and the options on the table are
"full access or nothing". moira exists to put a third option there.
